# Part 4: Tableau Executive Dashboard & Data Storytelling

## Business Insights

### Insight 1: Sales Trend
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
The business exhibits highly volatile month-over-month performance rather than a flat, predictable baseline. In particular, the late summer sales flipped completely between 2024 and 2025.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
In August 2024, sales hit a historic low of Rs. 6,303,794.69 with profits dropping to Rs. 885,649.73. In contrast, August 2025 recorded the absolute revenue peak for the entire dataset at Rs. 10,861,499.92 with a corresponding profit of Rs. 1,735,384.80. Revenue grew by 72.3% in August compared to the previous year.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
The dramatic swing in August performance suggests a highly effective operational or promotional turnaround. While Q3 2024 suffered from severe seasonal stagnation, Q3 2025 capitalized on heavy volume procurement potentially by means of aggressive marketing campaigns, strategic bulk client contracts, or other methods.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
The company must audit the operational playbook used in July/August 2025 to find what caused 10.8M sales surge. Then, this strategy should be institutionalized to address the recurrent historical lows in months like April (which dropped to Rs. 7.19M in 2025).
__________
### Insight 2: Regional Sales
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
Sales are heavily concentrated in a single state market, which acts as the main source of revenue.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
Rajasthan is the absolute revenue leader, generating Rs. 20,836,623.97 in sales. Rajasthan's sales volume is more than double that of major markets like Maharashtra (Rs. 8.99M) and Uttar Pradesh (Rs. 9.91M), while still maintaining a healthy profit margin of 15.76%.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
Rajasthan has achieved deep market penetration and exceptional brand equity. The fact that profit margins stay high despite the massive number of sales shows that local teams have won costumers without relying on predatory discounts.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
The company should conduct an immediate operational audit on the Rajasthan distribution and marketing to identify the key strategies contributing to this success and apply similar tactics in underperforming markets such as Maharashtra.
__________
### Insight 3: Regional Profits
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
Though larger states bring in the most total revenue, smaller volume markets operate at much higher efficiency, generating the most profit for every Rupee spent.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
Jharkhand (Sales: Rs. 8,722,895.09) and Karnataka (Sales: Rs. 12,537,577.45) lead in capital efficiency, with peak profit margins of 16.83% and 16.60% respectively.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
These two markets seem to be very profitable. This may be due to faster deliveries, higher-value orders, smoother supply chains, or customers who don’t necessarily require heavy discounts.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
The company should figure out why Jharkhand and Karnataka are outperforming other states. If it is due to lower regional shipping/warehousing costs, the company should consider increasing marketing investment here. Increasing sales volume in the most efficient markets will help overall profit quickly.
__________
### Insight 4: Category Profitability
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
There is a big imbalance in profitability across product categories. Technology is the main source of profit, whereas Furniture struggles to break even.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
The four sub-categories under Technology (Copiers, Accessories, Phones, Machines) create massive profit, each generating between Rs. 6.44M and Rs. 7.31M with steady profit margins around 18.00% to 18.49%. On the other hand, sub-categories under Furniture (Tables and Bookcases) barely got by with margins of just 5.67% (Profit: Rs. 731,533.65) and 5.71% (Profit: Rs. 711,733.14) respectively.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
The companies financial health relies heavily on Technology. The low profitability in Furniture suggests higher hidden costs, perhaps due to heavy manufacturing materials, expensive bulk logistics, limited price increases. The company is spending immense resources and efforts on Furniture despite marginal profit returns.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
Leadership should consider investing more resources toward more profitable categories. The company should focus on marketing toward high-margin sub-categories (especially Phones and Accessories). Simultaneously, the company needs to rethink Furniture commitments, either increasing retail prices, renegotiating shipping costs, or cutting out the lowest performing sub-categories.

### Insight 5: Customer Segment Performance
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
Sales distribution is heavily dependent on regional customer demographics, with the South region being the top primary consumer market, while the East and West being stronger in corporate and remote-work footprints.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
The South region outdoes all other regions across every single segment, generating a massive total volume of Rs. 64,693,706.73, led by the Consumer segment at Rs. 23,034,982.24. Conversely, in the East region, Home Office is the dominant sector at Rs. 17,546,295.98.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
The South has an incredibly strong retail customer base. The East and West regions have successfully led the Home Office segment, where order volumes are smaller per transaction but focused on higher-value enterprise.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
The company should align marketing spend with these regional strengths. Allocate promotional budgets toward the South. For the East and West regions, target marketing efforts toward corporate procurement channels.

### Insight 6: Discount vs Profit
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
The economic Standard Class shipping infrastructure is so congested where in the number of queued up orders cannot be handled with existing capacity leading the delayed delivery of most of the orders. Even the premium paid shipping options too are not meeting their expected pre-defined timelines.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
The major operational delay of more than 7 days encountered in case of 201 out of 203 orders and that of around 5-6 days encountered in case of 1141 out of 1224 orders, is contributing to overall 93% of the orders shipped by Standard Class. Similarly, the premium paid First Class and Same Day shipping options experienced delays of 3-4 days in case of 165 and 3 orders respectively.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
The shipping logistical services responsible for handling most of the orders by Standard Class option are least efficient, which pose a risk of losing our loyal and long-term customers. Though we charge premium in case of First Class and Same Day shipping options, but delivering like a standard class option; it poses the risk to the reputation of the organization leading to lose the business due to poor customer satisfaction reviews, cancellation of orders and higher number of refund requests raised.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
The organization must start working immediately towards improving the shipping logistics services by bring in new service providers and negotiating with strict terms and conditions regarding service-level agreements (SLA). Also, the internal operations audit should insist on implementing an auto-alert system that will notify the delivery team to prioritise the premium order in not considered for dispatch within few hours.

### Insight 7: Shipping Performance
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
The graph shows an inverse relationship between Discounts offered and Profits earned. With the increase in discount percentage, the profitability comes down significantly, and the performance tends impact negatively leading to losses with further discounts if continues.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
At the lowest discount (0%) level, the transactional profits are the highest at approximately Rs 133,000. As the discount reaches in the range of 25-30% the profit significantly shrinks to the range of Rs. 51,400 - 60,500, alongside losses down to Rs 17,800. At a discount of around 35%, the profits gets almost wiped out, the maximum gain being stuck at Rs 4,500.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
Such promotional discounting strategy completely collapses the organizational profitability. Though the Marketing division can offer some discounts at times to clear the inventories or to seek new deals, it can potentially ruin the corporate policy. The heavy discounts alongside fixed operational overhead costs severely outpace the highly discounted revenue. This strategy doesn’t help much towards the growth of the profitable organization.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
The organization must implement a corporate sales policy with maximum the discounts capped at 20% for the retail customers. Any deals requiring higher discounts should go through the management approvals with active mandatory margin protection.

### Insight 8: Return Analysis
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
The tendency to return products depends highly on the product category. The Office Supplies and Technology categories have low returns rates; whereas the Furniture are returning pretty high.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
Improvements are seen in all the sub-categories under Furniture as can be seen in cases of Chairs (7.99%), Furnishings (8.16%), and Bookcases (8.42%). At the same time, the high-volume categories like Phones (3.01%) and Copiers (2.72%) are returning relatively low.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
The Furniture division is in distress due to low margins. As the furniture are heavy and high-volume items, the cost of return deliveries impact is major. Together with low profit margins of 5-8% in case of Furniture category, with returns factored in, it can be seen that the organization is losing money on every Chair and Bookcase transaction.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
The internal audit should reveal the reason behind overall average return rates at organization level are just half that of Furniture. Find out if the furniture item returns are due to the mismatch with product images or dimensions, or damages due to mis-handling by the suppliers or incorrect descriptions listed on the sales portal. We should ensure to correct such things and improve on the quality of the services provided to customer satisfaction.

### Insight 9: Business Risk or Opportunity
1. **Observation from Dashboard:**  
By the combination of different dashboard metrics, it is observed that the organization health majorly depends on the success of only one product category. The risky operational inefficiencies of other product categories are left hidden behind.

2. **Data Evidence:**  
For example, the Technology as a category is generating the major and dependable profit margin of approximately 18%, contributing up to Rs. 7.31 million per sub-category. Whereas the Furniture as a category is delivering in the meagre margin of 5.67% to 8.25%, with peak return of 8.42%. Also, the shipping handled by Standard Class causing the delays to over 93% of the overall number of orders.

3. **Business Interpretation:**  
This exposes the organization to sudden & severe risks to operations. The super-efficient Technology team seems to be compensating for the low returns and heavy discount leakages within the Furniture category, and also the shipping delays occurring due to inefficient logistical infrastructure. Any slowdown in sales caused due to unforeseen market dynamics, can lead to drastic shrinking in overall profitability.

4. **Recommended Action:**  
The executive team must work on a strategy to quickly re-balance the product portfolio. More opportunities can be explored and capital infusion can be considered in the high-margin Technology category by expanding the portfolio to products like Phones and Accessories. Also, the low-margin Furniture category expansion efforts can be paused to mitigate the risk to profitability until a thorough all-inclusive audit reveal and fix the packaging and logistics related issues towards improving the efficiency.
