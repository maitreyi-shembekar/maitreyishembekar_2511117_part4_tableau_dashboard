# Part 4: Tableau Executive Dashboard & Data Storytelling

## Dashboard Story

### 1. Executive Summary
In the analysis of data from 2024 and 2025 together, the company made a huge comeback with 72.3% year-over-year revenue surge in August 2025. But the overall corporate stability is pretty weak. The net profitability relies too much on one category (Technology) and one single region (the South).

Meanwhile, the company is losing money because of three problems: shipping (systemic inefficiencies in logistics), giving away too many discounts (an unsustainable promotional discounting framework), and many people sending back items in the Furniture category (severe product return rates). For a safer growth in the long run, the company needs to stop trying to sell as much as possible (in volume) and fixing some operations.
_______
### 2. What is Performing Well
1. **Technology:** Technology hardware is the primary wealth generator. Across all four sub-categories (Copiers, Accessories, Phones, Machines), performance is strong and predictable, maintaining peak efficiency with profit margins consistently hovering between 18.00% and 18.49%, yielding profits up to Rs. 7.31M per line.

2. **Regional Volume (Sales) & Efficiency (Profit Margin):** Rajasthan is a commercial powerhouse, leading Rs. 20.83M in sales with 15.76% profitability margin. Smaller volume markets like Jharkhand and Karnataka are generating the highest profit margins in the country at 16.83% and 16.60% respectively.

3. **Southern Consumer:** The South region has successfully scaled its retail operations, generating Rs. 64.69M in aggregate sales, majorly by the Consumer segment (Rs. 23.03M).
________
### 3. What is Underperforming
1. **Furniture:** The Furniture category represents a major expenditure of operational energy for negligible return. Sub-categories like Tables and Bookcases have profit margins of 5.67% and 5.71%. This is beacuse of high operating costs. Furniture suffers from more return rates, led by Bookcases at 8.42% and Furnishings at 8.16%.

2. **Standard Class Logistics:** Standard Class shipping is the single largest point of operational inconsistensies, responsible for around 93% of all delayed orders nationwide (including 201 severe 7+ day delays and 1,141 moderate 5-6 day delays).
________
### 4. What Risks Are Visible
- The high-margin Technology division is quietly subsidizing the heavy logistics delays, high return rates, and thin pricing margins of the Furniture division. If Technology slows down due to an economic shift or supply chain disruption, profitability will reduce and cause more problems in other mentioned areas.

- There is an acute customer satisfaction risk in shipping tiers. The analysis recorded 165 First Class orders taking 3-4 days for delivery, and 3 Same Day orders failing their windows entirely. Charging customers premium pricing for standard delivery windows creates a severe compliance and brand-reputation liability.

- Scatter plot analysis shows an inverse relationship between discounting and net profit. When the discounts cross past 20% to 25%-35% range, profits decrease and losses increase up to -Rs. 17,800, proving that heavy discounting does not drive profitable scale.
_________
### 5. What Opportunities Are Visible
- While the South is dominated by consumers, the East and West regions show strong sales in the Home Office segment. This is an opportunity to deploy corporate procurement strategies for remote infrastructure.

-  States like Jharkhand and Karnataka show that the local logistics or product mixes are highly optimized. It is an opportunity to scale local marketing spend in these specific high-margin regions.
_________
### 6. Recommended Business Actions
1. **Enforce a Discount Limit:** Implement a corporate restriction by establishing a maximum discount limit of 20% for standard sales accounts. Any deal requiring a discount higher than 20% must be reviewed and a margin-protection assessment must be done.

2. **Restructure the Furniture Line:** Put a hold to expanding the furniture lines. Stiffen minimum retail pricing rates, update any online product details and dimensions to reduce return triggers, and require stricter quality control packaging from suppliers to eliminate transit damage.

3. **Improve Shipping Logistics:** Diversify third-party logistics providers for the economy tiers to clear the Standard Class problem. Integrate automated operational alerts in the warehouse: if a premium First Class or Same Day order is not dispatched within 12 hours, it must be automatically fast-tracked.

4. **Reallocate Capital to High-Yield Streams:** Reallocate a percentage of underperforming Furniture marketing budgets into high-margin Technology categories (specifically Phones and Accessories) and expand marketing in the East and West Home Office sectors.
________
### 7. Limitations of the Dashboard
While the dashboard provides visibility into historical sales and profits, it does not provide insight into underlying cost structures, meaning we cannot find out whether furniture margins are caused by what factors, like high suppliers costs or something else. The dashboard lacks real-time customer data, making it harder to determine the exact qualitative reasons behind high product return rates or specific premium shipping delays.
_________
### 8. Suggested Next Analysis
- A Shipping Cost Audit of logistical expenses to find out exactly why Standard Class is restricted and calculate the true cost of shipping heavy items versus light technology hardware.

- Run an analysis on customer sentiment and return triggers to identify patterns in why bookcases and furnishings are rejected at a higher rate than the rest of the catalog.
